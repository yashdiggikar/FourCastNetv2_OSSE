1.  CONTROL RUN RMSE BY LATITUDE SLICE (REFERENCE BASELINE)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The control run (no insertion) RMSE in the mature phase increases strongly
with latitude for all variables. This is the baseline against which all
insertion improvements are measured.

Z500 control RMSE (m²/s²):
  2°N  :   145.3    ← small-amplitude tropical troposphere
  14°N :   243.9    ← subtropical jet variability
  40°N : 1,358.5    ← storm-track baroclinic systems (9× larger than 2°N)
  60°N : 2,196.6    ← subpolar vortex + high-amplitude ridges/troughs

T500 control RMSE (K):
  2°N  :  1.07 K
  14°N :  2.04 K
  40°N :  5.79 K
  60°N :  7.94 K

U500 control RMSE (m/s):
  2°N  :  6.98    14°N : 9.20    40°N : 14.78    60°N : 15.49

Wind500 control RMSE (m/s):
  2°N  :  4.42    14°N : 5.79    40°N : 12.55    60°N : 12.49

Wind10 control RMSE (m/s):
  2°N  :  2.30    14°N : 2.70    40°N :  3.92    60°N :  3.63

Physical interpretation: The rapid increase in RMSE from 2°N to 40°N reflects
the dominance of baroclinic instability in extratropics — synoptic systems at
40°N have geopotential amplitudes of 500–1000 m (= 5000–10000 m²/s²), far
larger than tropical perturbations. The FCNv2 control run loses predictive
skill for these high-amplitude systems beyond ~2 weeks.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
2.  TEMPERATURE INSERTION (T-INS) — RESULTS BY SLICE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

T-insertion forces T500 = ERA5 truth at every step from day 30 onward.
T500 RMSE drops to exactly 0% at all slices (trivially, by construction).
Below are downstream effects on other variables.

2.1  U500 improvement:
  2°N  :  6.0%     ← near-zero — thermal wind breaks down at equator
  14°N : 34.5%     ← PEAK — subtropical jet is thermally driven
  40°N : 22.9%     ← lower than 14°N despite stronger baroclinicity
  60°N : 26.2%

  KEY FINDING: T-insertion improves U500 most at 14°N (subtropical jet),
  NOT at 40°N (storm-track). This is physically surprising. In classical QG
  theory, thermal wind coupling is ∂u/∂p = -(R/fp) ∂T/∂y. At 14°N, the
  Coriolis parameter f is smaller than at 40°N, which would normally WEAKEN
  thermal-wind coupling. The observed strong response at 14°N suggests FCNv2
  represents the subtropical jet as primarily thermally driven (consistent with
  its role as the upper-level expression of the Hadley circulation), while the
  40°N jet is more eddy-driven (less sensitive to T500 alone).

2.2  Z500 improvement:
  2°N  : 18.6%     ← weak — tropical Z500 not strongly linked to T500 gradient
  14°N : 37.9%
  40°N : 39.8%     ← PEAK — midlatitude hypsometric coupling strongest
  60°N : 36.8%

  Z500 improvement is roughly uniform (37–40%) from 14°N to 60°N, with a clear
  drop at 2°N (18.6%). This is consistent with hypsometric balance
  (ΔZ = (R/g)ΔT·ln(p₁/p₂)) which holds in the geostrophic regime (14°N–60°N)
  but breaks down near the equator.

2.3  Wind500 improvement — NOTABLE NEGATIVE VALUE:
  2°N  : -3.0%     ← T-INSERTION WORSENS WIND500 AT 2°N
  14°N : 19.4%
  40°N : 21.3%
  60°N : 18.0%

  The -3.0% at 2°N is the most physically significant result of this analysis.
  Inserting corrected T500 at the equator creates a temperature field that is
  inconsistent with the FCNv2 wind field (which was balanced to the wrong T500).
  This imbalance generates ageostrophic circulations (gravity wave responses)
  that temporarily increase wind500 RMSE — classic "analysis shock."
  At higher latitudes (≥14°N) where f > 0, geostrophic adjustment is rapid
  and the model recovers; at the equator, the adjustment timescale diverges
  (f→0) and the imbalance persists through the mature phase.

2.4  Wind10 improvement — REVERSED LATITUDE GRADIENT:
  2°N  : 22.6%     ← PEAK — opposite pattern to all other variables
  14°N : 21.8%
  40°N :  7.6%     ← weak
  60°N :  5.5%     ← weakest

  T-insertion improves near-surface winds most in the tropics.
  Physical mechanism: tropical surface trade winds are thermally driven via the
  Hadley circulation. Correcting T500 modifies the upper-level thermal gradient
  that drives the tropical meridional overturning, which in turn adjusts the
  surface easterlies. In midlatitudes, surface winds are controlled by boundary
  layer processes and extratropical cyclone dynamics, not by 500 hPa temperature.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
3.  WIND INSERTION (WIND-INS) — RESULTS BY SLICE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Wind-insertion forces U500 = V500 = ERA5 truth at every step from day 30.
U500 and Wind500 RMSE → 0% trivially. Downstream effects:

3.1  T500 improvement:
  2°N  : 32.5%
  14°N : 57.0%
  40°N : 71.2%     ← PEAK — strong thermal advection in storm tracks
  60°N : 61.0%

  Clear latitude gradient: tropical T500 is less responsive to wind correction
  (32.5%) than midlatitude T500 (71.2%). This confirms the direction of the
  dominant coupling in FCNv2: in midlatitudes, temperature is advected by winds
  (∂T/∂t ≈ -u·∇T), so correct winds immediately improve T500. In the tropics,
  temperature anomalies are driven by convection and radiative processes,
  making them less sensitive to horizontal wind correction.

3.2  Z500 improvement:
  2°N  : 33.5%
  14°N : 53.7%
  40°N : 84.8%     ← PEAK — storm-track baroclinic instability corrected
  60°N : 72.8%

  The 84.8% improvement at 40°N is the single largest downstream improvement
  across all experiments and all slices. Wind-insertion at the storm-track
  centre (40°N) corrects the geopotential height field by 85%, meaning the
  large-scale circulation pattern at 40°N is almost entirely determined by
  the correctness of the wind field. This confirms that FCNv2's Z500 errors
  at 40°N are primarily caused by incorrect wind shear (baroclinic errors),
  not by errors in mass distribution (direct Z500 bias).

3.3  Wind10 improvement:
  2°N  : 45.5%
  14°N : 53.7%
  40°N : 62.0%
  60°N : 61.0%

  Wind-insertion propagates efficiently to near-surface winds at all latitudes,
  with a modest increase from tropics to midlatitudes. This suggests the FCNv2
  model has learned a strong vertical coupling between 500 hPa winds and 10m
  winds, consistent with the barotropic component of midlatitude flow.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
4.  GEOPOTENTIAL INSERTION (Z-INS) — RESULTS BY SLICE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Z-insertion forces Z500 = ERA5 truth at every step from day 30.
Z500 RMSE → 0% trivially. Downstream effects:

4.1  T500 improvement — STRONGEST LATITUDE GRADIENT OF ALL EXPERIMENTS:
  2°N  : 16.1%     ← weakest
  14°N : 46.8%
  40°N : 80.8%
  60°N : 83.5%     ← PEAK

  The range 16% → 84% across slices is the largest latitude gradient
  observed in any experiment. This directly quantifies the breakdown of the
  hypsometric relationship Z500 → T̄_layer as latitude decreases.

  At 40°N and 60°N, inserting correct Z500 fixes ~82% of T500 error — Z500
  almost completely encodes the layer-mean temperature through hypsometric
  balance: T̄ = (g/R) · ΔZ / ln(p₁/p₂).

  At 2°N, only 16% of T500 error is fixed by correct Z500 — the tropical
  temperature structure is dominated by moist convection and latent heating,
  processes that operate independently of the geopotential height field.
  In the tropics, Z500 is nearly constant (weak horizontal gradients), so it
  contains little information about temperature anomalies.

4.2  U500 improvement:
  2°N  : 35.4%
  14°N : 58.3%
  40°N : 70.7%
  60°N : 69.5%

  Geostrophic wind: u_g = -(g/f)·∂Z/∂y. At 2°N, f → 0 so even perfect Z500
  cannot recover geostrophic winds (the geostrophic relation diverges).
  Despite this, Z-insertion still achieves 35.4% at 2°N — this residual
  improvement likely comes from ageostrophic components (gradient wind
  balance) that FCNv2 has learned empirically from training data.

4.3  Wind500 improvement:
  2°N  : 20.9%
  14°N : 41.6%
  40°N : 66.6%
  60°N : 64.4%

4.4  Wind10 improvement:
  2°N  : 37.5%
  14°N : 47.0%
  40°N : 54.4%
  60°N : 54.5%

6.  KEY SCIENTIFIC FINDINGS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Finding 1 — Analysis Shock at the Equator:
  T-insertion WORSENS Wind500 at 2°N by 3.0%. This is the clearest evidence
  of analysis shock in the FCNv2 system. The model cannot maintain thermal-wind
  balance near the equator, so inserting correct T500 into a wind field
  balanced to wrong T500 creates ageostrophic disturbances. This is not seen at
  any other slice or in any other experiment.

Finding 2 — Z500 is the Master Variable in the Extratropics:
  Z-insertion achieves T500 improvements of 80–84% at 40°N and 60°N, and U500
  improvements of 70–71% at the same latitudes. Z500 encodes almost all the
  information needed to reconstruct the extratropical thermal and wind structure
  through hypsometric balance and geostrophic balance respectively.
  In the deep tropics this breaks down completely (T500 only 16% from Z-ins).

Finding 3 — FCNv2's Dominant Coupling Direction is Wind → Temperature at Midlatitudes:
  Wind-insertion achieves higher non-trivial T500 improvements than T-insertion
  achieves U500 improvements at 40°N (71.2% vs 22.9%). This asymmetry shows
  FCNv2 is more capable of learning the wind → T500 pathway (temperature
  advection) than the T500 → wind pathway (thermal wind balance inversion).

Finding 4 — The Subtropical Jet is Thermally Driven in FCNv2:
  T-insertion peaks at 14°N for U500 (34.5%), exceeding the midlatitude
  response (40°N: 22.9%). FCNv2 has learned that subtropical upper-level
  winds are determined by the meridional temperature gradient (Hadley cell
  thermal forcing), consistent with the thermally-direct nature of the
  subtropical jet.

Finding 5 — T-insertion Improves Tropical Surface Winds (Hadley Circulation):
  T-insertion Wind10 improvement is highest at 2°N (22.6%) and 14°N (21.8%),
  and lowest at 40°N (7.6%) and 60°N (5.5%). This reveals that FCNv2 has
  learned the Hadley circulation pathway: upper-level tropical temperature
  anomalies drive meridional overturning that affects the surface trade winds.
  This coupling does not operate in the midlatitudes.

Finding 6 — Wind-insertion Z500 Peaks at the Storm Track (40°N):
  84.8% Z500 improvement at 40°N is the single largest downstream improvement
  in the entire analysis. It directly identifies baroclinic instability in
  the storm-track region as the primary source of FCNv2 forecast error: the
  model cannot sustain correct circulation patterns at 40°N because it cannot
  correctly simulate baroclinic growth, but given correct winds as input,
  it largely recovers the large-scale geopotential structure.

7.  COMPARISON WITH BROAD-BAND RESULTS (lats/ directory)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The broad-band analysis (Tropics/Midlatitudes/Polar) averages over wide ranges
and obscures the within-band variation revealed here. Key differences:

  Broad Tropics vs 2°N vs 14°N:
    - Broad Tropics = (|lat|<30°) mixes the 2°N deep tropics signal with the
      14°N subtropical signal. The -3.0% Wind500 analysis shock at 2°N is
      completely hidden in the Broad Tropics average.
    - Z-ins T500 improvement: Broad Tropics = 56.2%, but 2°N = 16.1% and
      14°N = 46.8%. The subtropical average pulls the tropical mean up
      significantly, masking the near-zero response at the equator.

  Broad Midlatitudes vs 40°N:
    - Broad Midlatitudes averages 30°–60°N both hemispheres. The 40°N storm
      track signature is diluted. Wind-ins Z500 = 83.2% (broad) vs 84.8% (40°N)
      — very close because 40°N is the dominant contributor.

  Broad Polar vs 60°N:
    - The 60°N slice covers 55°–65°, near the edge of the Polar band (>60°).
      Results are broadly similar between Polar band and 60°N slice.

Recommendation for thesis: use the slice analysis to discuss specific dynamical
mechanisms (storm track, subtropical jet, equatorial dynamics) and the broad-band
analysis for the overall summary bar chart.
