Coupling score C(A→B):
  For each pressure level of response group B:
      pct_level = 100 × (RMSE_ctrl − RMSE_ins_A) / RMSE_ctrl   [mature phase mean]
  Then:
      C(A→B) = mean(pct_level)  across all 13 pressure levels (1000–50 hPa)

Positive = inserting A reduces error in B (A→B causal coupling).
Negative = inserting A worsens B (analysis shock).
Trivial (self-inserted) cells ≈ 100% by construction — excluded from analysis.

2.  TEMPERATURE INSERTION — COUPLING RESULTS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

T-insertion forces all 13 T levels to ERA5 truth from day 30.

2.1  T-Ins → U (18.0% avg, peak 31.6% at 200 hPa, min 5.4% at 50 hPa):

  The thermal-wind balance (∂u/∂p = -(R/fp)·∂T/∂y) links vertical wind shear
  to meridional temperature gradients. Inserting correct T should improve U
  through this balance. However, only 18% average improvement is achieved.

  The peak at 200 hPa (upper troposphere/jet level) is physically meaningful:
  the subtropical and polar jets are maintained by the meridional temperature
  gradient between tropics and poles. Correcting the temperature field at this
  level allows the model to partially recover the jet wind speed.

  The weak response at 50 hPa (5.4%) reflects that stratospheric winds in FCNv2
  are driven primarily by planetary wave dynamics rather than thermal gradients
  in the data-assimilation sense.

2.2  T-Ins → Z (29.9% avg, peak 44.2% at 250 hPa, min 12.1% at 925 hPa):

  The hypsometric relationship (ΔZ = (R/g)·T̄·ln(p₁/p₂)) directly links
  layer-mean temperature to geopotential thickness. This is the clearest
  physical coupling from T→Z. The 29.9% average improvement confirms the
  relationship is encoded in FCNv2.

  Peak at 250 hPa (tropopause level): Z250 is most sensitive to the temperature
  of the tropospheric column, where T corrections have the most mass.
  Minimum at 925 hPa (12.1%): near-surface Z is controlled by boundary-layer
  thermal structures not captured by T500 insertion alone.

2.3  T-Ins → R (6.4% avg, peak 49.3% at 50 hPa, NEGATIVE -12.0% at 925 hPa):

  Humidity is the least responsive variable to T insertion. The 6.4% average
  masks strong vertical variation: a large improvement in the stratosphere
  (49.3% at 50 hPa) but actual degradation near the surface (-12.0% at 925 hPa).

  The negative value at 925 hPa is an analysis shock signature: correcting T
  near the surface changes the saturation mixing ratio and disrupts the
  boundary-layer moisture balance that FCNv2 had established for the wrong T field.
  This is the same imbalance mechanism as the equatorial Wind500 degradation seen
  in the lat-slice analysis (Section 5.7).

KEY FINDING: T-insertion is the weakest driver in FCNv2. Its strongest
non-trivial coupling is to Z500 (29.9%) via hypsometric balance. U and V
improvements are modest (16–18%), and humidity barely responds (6.4%).
This confirms that FCNv2 treats temperature as a consequence of dynamics,
not a cause of it.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
3.  WIND INSERTION — COUPLING RESULTS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Wind-insertion forces all 13 U and V levels to ERA5 truth from day 30.

3.1  Wind-Ins → Z (71.8% avg, peak 82.9% at 700 hPa, min 31.7% at 50 hPa):

  The strongest non-trivial coupling in the entire matrix. Correct winds restore
  71.8% of Z-field errors on average across all pressure levels.

  Peak at 700 hPa (lower-mid troposphere, 82.9%): this is the level where
  baroclinic instability and synoptic-scale wave growth are most active.
  The mid-tropospheric Z field (ridges and troughs) is almost entirely determined
  by the wind field's divergence and vorticity. With correct winds, the model
  reconstructs the geopotential height pattern almost perfectly at this level.

  Minimum at 50 hPa (31.7%): stratospheric Z is driven by planetary-wave forcing
  from below and radiative processes; tropospheric wind correction propagates only
  partially to the stratosphere.

3.2  Wind-Ins → T (55.4% avg, peak 69.2% at 200 hPa, min 6.2% at 50 hPa):

  Horizontal temperature advection (∂T/∂t ≈ -u·∇T) is the dominant temperature
  tendency in the free troposphere. With correct winds, FCNv2 accurately advects
  warm and cold air masses, recovering 55.4% of T errors on average.

  Peak at 200 hPa (69.2%): upper-tropospheric temperature advection by the jet
  stream is particularly efficient. The jet brings warm subtropical air poleward
  and cold polar air equatorward; correct winds allow this to be simulated accurately.

  The gradient from upper troposphere to stratosphere (200 hPa: 69.2% →
  50 hPa: 6.2%) shows that stratospheric T is controlled by processes (radiative,
  wave-driven descent) that do not respond to wind correction at lower levels.

3.3  Wind-Ins → R (43.1% avg, peak 53.8% at 250 hPa, min 8.4% at 50 hPa):

  Humidity responds substantially to wind correction (43.1%) — much more than
  to T correction (6.4%). This reflects moisture advection: the model transports
  water vapour correctly when winds are correct. Peak at 250 hPa (upper
  tropospheric humidity) where cross-jet moisture transport is important.

KEY FINDING: Wind insertion is the most powerful single-variable correction in
FCNv2. It dominates Z recovery (71.8%) and substantially improves T (55.4%)
and R (43.1%). The high Wind→Z coupling confirms that FCNv2's primary forecast
errors originate from incorrect representation of the horizontal circulation.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
4.  GEOPOTENTIAL INSERTION — COUPLING RESULTS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Geo-insertion forces all 13 Z levels to ERA5 truth from day 30.

4.1  Geo-Ins → T (65.8% avg, peak 77.8% at 500 hPa, min 23.0% at 50 hPa):

  The highest T coupling of any non-trivial experiment. Z500 encodes layer-mean
  temperature almost completely through hypsometric balance. The peak precisely
  at 500 hPa (77.8%) reflects that Z500 is the thickness integral of T from the
  surface to 500 hPa — correcting Z500 directly corrects the mid-tropospheric
  temperature column.

  Unlike T-Ins, which improves Z only partially (29.9%), Geo-Ins improves T
  much more (65.8%). This asymmetry — strong Z→T but weak T→Z — reflects
  the causal direction: in quasi-geostrophic theory, geopotential gradients
  drive wind and temperature, not the reverse.

4.2  Geo-Ins → U (65.4% avg, peak 66.6% at 250 hPa, min 62.5% at 50 hPa):

  The most vertically uniform coupling in the entire analysis — U responds to
  Z correction with nearly constant 62–67% improvement across all pressure levels.
  This is the clearest signature of geostrophic balance operating at all levels:
      u_g = -(g/f) · ∂Z/∂y

  The uniformity is physically significant: it means FCNv2 has learned geostrophic
  balance consistently throughout the atmosphere from the boundary layer to the
  stratosphere. This is a validation of the model's large-scale dynamics.

4.3  Geo-Ins → V (68.1% avg):

  V coupling to Z is slightly stronger than U (68.1% vs 65.4%), reflecting the
  dominant meridional pressure gradient in the Northern Hemisphere winter pattern.
  The geostrophic v-wind (v_g = (g/f)·∂Z/∂x) is well-encoded in the model.

4.4  Geo-Ins → R (28.4% avg, peak 41.9% at 200 hPa, min 3.9% at 50 hPa):

  Humidity responds moderately to Z correction. The mechanism is indirect:
  correcting Z recovers the geostrophic wind (U/V), which then advects moisture
  correctly. The chain Z→Wind→R is less efficient than direct Wind→R.
  Peak at 200 hPa: upper-level divergence associated with correct Z patterns
  drives vertical motion which modulates upper-tropospheric humidity.

KEY FINDING: Geo-insertion achieves the most balanced, vertically uniform
improvement across variables. The U/V coupling (65–68%) is almost identical
to the T coupling (65.8%), confirming that Z500 simultaneously encodes the
thermal structure (hypsometric) and the wind structure (geostrophic) of the
atmosphere in FCNv2's learned representation.

5.  CROSS-EXPERIMENT COMPARISON AND CAUSAL HIERARCHY
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

5.1  Best coupling per response variable:

  Response  │  Best experiment  │  Score   │  Physical mechanism
  ──────────┼───────────────────┼──────────┼───────────────────────────────────
  T         │  Geo-Ins          │  65.8%   │  Hypsometric: Z encodes T̄
  U         │  Geo-Ins          │  65.4%   │  Geostrophic: u_g = -(g/f)∂Z/∂y
  V         │  Geo-Ins          │  68.1%   │  Geostrophic: v_g = (g/f)∂Z/∂x
  Z         │  Wind-Ins         │  71.8%   │  Baroclinic: divergence/vorticity
  R         │  Wind-Ins         │  43.1%   │  Moisture advection: u·∇q

  Geo-Ins best for T, U, V.  Wind-Ins best for Z, R.
  T-Ins best for none.

5.2  The coupling asymmetry: Wind→Z vs Z→Wind

  Wind-Ins → Z = 71.8%  vs  Geo-Ins → U/V = 65–68%

  Wind-insertion improves Z slightly better than geo-insertion improves winds.
  This suggests that in FCNv2, the wind field is slightly more fundamental
  than the geopotential field — winds drive Z through divergence/convergence,
  and this pathway is stronger than Z driving winds through geostrophic balance.
  However, the values are close (71.8% vs 65–68%), indicating near-symmetric
  coupling consistent with quasi-geostrophic dynamics.

5.3  T-insertion asymmetry:

  T-Ins → Z = 29.9%   but   Z-Ins → T = 65.8%   (2.2× stronger in reverse)
  T-Ins → U = 18.0%   but   U is rarely a driver of T in FCNv2

  The T→Z pathway (hypsometric: T determines geopotential thickness) is much
  weaker in FCNv2 than the Z→T pathway. This is counterintuitive — physically,
  T and Z are coupled symmetrically through thermodynamics. The asymmetry reveals
  that FCNv2 has learned Z as more predictive of T than vice versa, consistent
  with the model having learned to propagate the Z field quasi-geostrophically
  without explicitly preserving thermal-wind balance when T is perturbed.

5.4  Humidity (R) — consistently least responsive:

  T-Ins → R:    6.4%  (lowest in entire matrix, with negative values near surface)
  Wind-Ins → R: 43.1%
  Geo-Ins → R:  28.4%

  Relative humidity is the most independent variable in FCNv2's state space.
  Even with perfect wind correction, R errors are only reduced by 43%. This
  suggests FCNv2's moisture physics (convection, precipitation, cloud processes)
  operates semi-independently of the dynamical state variables. This is a known
  limitation of deterministic NWP models where moisture parameterization is not
  tightly coupled to the large-scale flow.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
6.  KEY SCIENTIFIC FINDINGS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Finding 1 — Wind is the dominant driver in FCNv2:
  Wind-insertion achieves the highest Z coupling (71.8%), the highest T coupling
  among non-trivial experiments (55.4%), and the highest R coupling (43.1%).
  FCNv2's forecast errors originate primarily from the horizontal circulation.

Finding 2 — Geopotential is the richest single-variable corrector:
  Geo-insertion achieves the most uniform improvement (65–68% for T, U, V),
  confirming that Z simultaneously encodes thermal and wind structure through
  hypsometric and geostrophic balance. Correcting Z restores the entire state.

Finding 3 — Temperature is a consequence, not a cause:
  T-Ins achieves only 29.9% Z coupling vs 65.8% Z→T coupling (Geo-Ins).
  The T→dynamics pathway is 2.2× weaker than the dynamics→T pathway in FCNv2.

Finding 4 — Analysis shock in humidity near the surface:
  T-Ins → R at 925 hPa = -12.0%. Inserting correct T disrupts the FCNv2
  boundary-layer moisture balance, degrading surface humidity prediction.

Finding 5 — Geostrophic balance is vertically consistent:
  Geo-Ins → U/V coupling is nearly uniform across all 13 levels (62.5–66.6%).
  FCNv2 has learned geostrophic balance from boundary layer to stratosphere.

Finding 6 — Wind→Z pathway is slightly stronger than Z→Wind:
  71.8% (Wind→Z) vs 65–68% (Z→Wind), suggesting wind field primacy in FCNv2's
  learned dynamics. The small asymmetry points to the model being more "wind-first"
  than strictly quasi-geostrophic.
