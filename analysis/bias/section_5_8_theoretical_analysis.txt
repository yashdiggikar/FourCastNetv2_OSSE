1. CONTROL-RUN BIAS DIAGNOSIS: WHAT THE FREE-RUNNING FCN DOES WRONG
================================================================================

1.1  The dominant failure: geopotential height drift (Z500)
--------------------------------------------------------------
The most striking result in the control run is the Z500 mean absolute bias of
588 m2/s2 (approximately 60 geopotential meters, converting by g = 9.80665 m/s2).
This represents roughly 1.1% of the climatological Z500 value (~55,000 m2/s2 in
the extratropics during boreal spring), accumulated over 30–60 days of free
autoregressive integration. The extremes are even more revealing: single-grid-
point biases reach -9,389 m2/s2 (about -957 m of geopotential height) and
+4,330 m2/s2 (+441 m). The negative extreme is more than twice as large as the
positive extreme.

This asymmetry is not random. It is physically interpretable: in NWP the
geopotential Z500 is bounded below by the surface (Z500 can only drop so much
before the atmosphere becomes gravitationally unstable) but is not symmetrically
bounded above. The model is therefore developing regions of anomalously deep
troughs (spuriously low geopotential) in the mature phase. These correspond to
quasi-stationary, amplified cyclonic anomalies that the model's autoregressive
dynamics generate and sustain without the restoring forces that act in the real
atmosphere (nonlinear wave breaking, baroclinic life-cycle termination, Rossby
wave dispersion).

The mean Z500 bias is -144.7 m2/s2, meaning the model's atmosphere is on average
~14.7 m too low in geopotential height in the mature phase. This is consistent
with a slight systematic cold bias in the thermal column (observed T500 mean bias
= -0.52 K), since a colder atmosphere has lower geopotential heights through the
hypsometric relationship. However, the mean bias is only a small fraction of the
MAB (144 vs. 588), indicating that the dominant Z500 error is spatial: the model
displaces ridges and troughs rather than uniformly cooling/warming the atmosphere.
This is a phase error, not a magnitude error — classic behavior of autoregressive
ML models that lose phase coherence with the real atmospheric circulation after
~2 weeks.

1.2  Wind field biases: loss of large-scale flow structure
-----------------------------------------------------------
The U500 MAB of 4.68 m/s and V500 MAB of 3.37 m/s are large relative to the
typical magnitude of these fields at 500 hPa (~15–25 m/s in the jet streams).
The mean biases are near-zero (-0.03 and +0.04 m/s respectively), confirming
again that errors are structural (spatial displacement of jets, troughs) rather
than systematic amplitude biases.

The Wind500 MAB of 3.47 m/s with a mean bias of -1.01 m/s indicates a slight
systematic underestimation of wind speeds in the mature phase. This is consistent
with the well-documented tendency of ML weather models to produce smoother,
lower-energy fields in long integrations — a consequence of the L2 loss function
used during training, which penalizes sharp spatial gradients and implicitly
damps high-wavenumber variance. Over 120 autoregressive steps, this spectral
smoothing accumulates into a measurable weakening of the mid-tropospheric flow.

1.3  Temperature bias: shallow drift with deep dynamical consequences
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The T500 MAB of 2.20 K is modest in absolute terms but consequential in its
dynamical implications. The range of the T500 bias (min = -26.2 K, max = +22.1 K)
reveals that the model has severely misplaced the thermal structure of the
atmosphere in specific regions (likely near strong fronts or the poleward edge of
the subtropical jet) while maintaining a reasonable global mean. A 26 K local
cold bias at 500 hPa would correspond to a misplacement of the polar vortex edge
or a collapsed troposphere in the high-latitude region — physically extreme states
that the real atmosphere cannot sustain due to radiative and dynamical constraints
the ML model does not enforce.

================================================================================
2. PHYSICAL COUPLING ANALYSIS: WHAT THE ML MODEL HAS LEARNED
================================================================================

The insertion experiments serve as perturbation probes of the model's internal
physics. By replacing one variable with truth while leaving all others free, we
isolate the cross-variable coupling that FCNv2 has learned from the ERA5 training
data. The following subsections interpret each experiment in terms of the physical
balance relationships of the mid-troposphere.

2.1  Hypsometric relationship and the T→Z coupling chain
---------------------------------------------------------
The hypsometric equation states:
  Z(p2) - Z(p1) = -(R/g) * integral from p1 to p2 of T d(ln p)

where R is the gas constant for dry air and g = 9.80665 m/s2. This means that
the geopotential height at a given pressure level is determined by the vertically
integrated temperature of the atmospheric column below it. At 500 hPa, Z500 is
set primarily by the mean temperature of the 1000–500 hPa layer.

The T-insertion experiment (inserting correct T500 at every step) reduces the
Z500 MAB by 40.3%: from 588.4 to 351.3 m2/s2. This is direct empirical evidence
that FCNv2 has internalized a version of the hypsometric relationship. When the
500 hPa temperature is anchored to truth, the model's Z500 field improves because
the model uses T500 information to diagnose Z500 through its learned equivalent
of the hypsometric integral.

However, the 59.7% residual Z500 error after T500 insertion reveals an important
limitation: FCNv2's T→Z coupling is single-level. The real hypsometric equation
integrates temperature across the entire column. Correcting T only at 500 hPa
while leaving T at 300, 700, and 1000 hPa uncorrected still leaves large column-
integrated temperature errors that manifest as Z500 bias. FCNv2 has apparently
not learned to extrapolate the single-level temperature correction to other levels
through baroclinic consistency — it knows T500 relates to Z500, but not how
much correction at 500 hPa should propagate to constrain Z at adjacent levels.

2.2  Geostrophic balance: the Z→Wind coupling
----------------------------------------------
Geostrophic balance in pressure coordinates reads:
  U_g = -(1/f) * (1/a) * dZ/dlambda
  V_g = +(1/f) * (1/a) * dZ/dphi

where f is the Coriolis parameter, a is Earth's radius, and lambda, phi are
longitude and latitude. This relationship states that the geopotential gradient
determines the geostrophic wind.

The Z-insertion experiment reduces U500 bias by 72.0% and V500 bias by 80.2%.
These numbers are a direct measurement of the "geostrophic fraction" of FCNv2's
wind field in the mature phase: approximately 72–80% of the wind error is
attributable to the wrong geopotential, and the remaining 20–28% represents
ageostrophic wind errors that persist even when Z500 is correct.

The 20–28% ageostrophic residual is physically sensible. At 500 hPa in the
extratropics, ageostrophic motions are associated with: (1) jet streak divergence
and convergence, (2) frontal circulations, (3) upper-level divergence associated
with precipitation systems, and (4) inertial oscillations. These processes are
dynamically important but not captured by linear geostrophy. The fact that FCNv2
retains approximately 20–28% ageostrophic wind error even when Z500 is perfect
suggests that the model has learned some ageostrophic dynamics (otherwise this
residual would be larger), but cannot generate the full spectrum of ageostrophic
motions from a geopotential boundary condition alone.

This 20–28% ageostrophic fraction can also be understood architecturally: the
AFNO model sees all 73 channels simultaneously and can, in principle, compute any
function of the input state. The fact that Z500→Wind correction is 72–80% and not
100% means either (a) the model genuinely learned the physical ageostrophic
motions that a geostrophically-initialized atmosphere generates, or (b) the
remaining wind errors are at small spatial scales that the model's spectral
representation poorly resolves even with a correct large-scale Φ. Both
interpretations suggest a fundamental limitation: true ageostrophic dynamics
requires time integration of the primitive equations, which the ML model
approximates in a single forward pass.

2.3  Thermal wind balance: the T→Wind coupling
-----------------------------------------------
Thermal wind balance is the vertical derivative of geostrophic balance:
  dV_g/d(ln p) = (R/f) * k_hat x grad_p(T)

This says that the vertical shear of the geostrophic wind is proportional to the
horizontal temperature gradient at that level. Inserting correct T500 reduces
U500 MAB by 20.7% and V500 MAB by 30.2%. These numbers measure the "thermal
wind fraction" of FCNv2's wind error.

The fact that T500 insertion improves winds less than Z500 insertion (21–30% vs.
72–80%) reveals the hierarchy: FCNv2 represents the geostrophic balance
(Z→Wind) more accurately than the thermal wind balance (T→Wind). This makes
physical sense because thermal wind relates temperature gradients at one level
to the wind shear across a finite vertical distance, requiring multi-level
consistency. The model can use the large-scale Z500 pattern (set by the full
column temperature) better than it can use the single-level temperature gradient.

2.4  Wind-driven geopotential: the reverse coupling
----------------------------------------------------
The Z-insertion experiment reveals the hierarchy most clearly: Z500→T500 reduction
is 88.2%, Z500→Wind500 reduction is 70.5%. Compare to T-insertion's Z500 reduction
of 40.3%. This tells us that in FCNv2:

  Z500 → T500 and Wind500  (strong, 70–88% correction)
  Wind → Z500              (moderate, 65% correction)
  T500 → Z500              (weaker, 40% correction)

The wind-insertion's Z500 correction of 65% is the most physically interesting
cross-coupling. It reflects two distinct physical pathways:

(a) Horizontal advection of geopotential:
    dZ/dt ~ -V . grad(Z)
    Correct winds directly advect the geopotential field correctly, reducing the
    spatial phase errors that dominate the Z500 bias.

(b) Vorticity forcing of geopotential:
    The omega-equation links vertical motion (and hence mass convergence/divergence)
    to the geopotential tendency:
    nabla^2(Z_tendency) ~ f * dVort/dp + ...
    Correct U, V fields generate better vorticity patterns, which drive better
    geopotential tendencies.

The 35% residual Z500 error after wind insertion (204 m2/s2 out of 588 m2/s2)
suggests that these advection and vorticity-forcing pathways account for only
65% of Z500 error generation. The remaining 35% arises from thermodynamic
sources: incorrect diabatic heating, radiative cooling, and latent heat release
that the model computes from the (uncorrected) temperature and moisture fields.
FCNv2 does not have explicit representations of these processes; they are
embedded in the neural network weights as statistical patterns. When T500 is
wrong (in the wind-insertion run), the model's implicit diabatic parameterization
also generates errors, which propagate into Z500.

2.5  The geopotential as master variable: emergent hierarchy
------------------------------------------------------------
Ranking insertion experiments by their total cross-variable correction power:

  Z-insertion  : corrects T500 88%, U500 72%, V500 80%, Wind500 71%  → total ~331%
  Wind-insertion: corrects T500 41%, Z500 65%                         → total ~106%
  T-insertion  : corrects U500 21%, V500 30%, Z500 40%, Wind500 14%  → total ~105%

The Z500 field is the dominant control variable in FCNv2's dynamics. This is
consistent with quasi-geostrophic theory, where Φ (or the streamfunction ψ = Φ/f)
determines the entire flow field through the elliptic inversion:
  nabla^2(ψ) + beta * d(ψ)/dy = PV_anomaly

In quasi-geostrophic dynamics, potential vorticity (PV) can be inverted to
recover the entire balanced state (Φ, T, U, V) at all levels from a single
scalar field. FCNv2 has effectively learned this inversion approximately: given
correct Z500 at one level, it reconstructs temperature (88%) and winds (72–80%)
with high fidelity. This is a remarkable emergent result — the model has discovered
from data alone that Z500 is a sufficient statistics for the mid-tropospheric state.

================================================================================
3. WHAT FCNv2 DOES NOT UNDERSTAND: PHYSICAL PROCESSES ABSENT FROM THE LEARNED
   DYNAMICS
================================================================================

3.1  Potential vorticity conservation
--------------------------------------
In adiabatic, frictionless flow, Ertel potential vorticity:
  PV = (f + zeta_theta) / delta_theta

is materially conserved following the three-dimensional flow. PV conservation
is the fundamental constraint that prevents the atmosphere from developing
arbitrary circulation patterns — it forces the flow to stay on PV isosurfaces.

FCNv2's training loss does not include any PV conservation constraint. Over 120
autoregressive steps, the model's state vector drifts off the manifold of
physically realizable PV-conserving flows. The growing Z500 bias (reaching 9,389
m2/s2 in some locations) is a manifestation of this drift: the model develops
spurious PV anomalies that would require diabatic heating rates far exceeding
anything in the real atmosphere to generate. A physical model would resist these
states through PV constraints; FCNv2 accumulates them step by step.

3.2  Geostrophic adjustment
----------------------------
When an imbalance exists between the wind and pressure fields (e.g., after an
observation is inserted), the atmosphere responds by radiating inertia-gravity
waves that carry away the imbalance and restore geostrophic balance on an inertial
timescale tau ~ 1/f ~ 12 hours at mid-latitudes. This process is called
geostrophic adjustment (Rossby, 1938; Blumen, 1972).

FCNv2 performs no geostrophic adjustment. When T-insertion creates a thermal
imbalance (correct T500 but wrong Z500), the model simply maps forward with
this inconsistent state without any balance restoration. The imbalance either
persists or triggers spurious responses that may worsen other variables. This is
the mechanism behind the 9.57% "temp-insertion worst" failure mode: in those
regions, the imbalanced state created by T-only insertion causes the model to
adjust Z500 in the wrong direction because the adjustment process requires
the full multivariate state (not just T500) to compute correctly.

This absence of adjustment also explains why Z-insertion is so powerful: Z500 is
the "balanced variable" — inserting it gives the model the most complete balanced
state possible at one level, minimizing the spurious adjustment responses.

3.3  Nonlinear wave-wave interactions and energy cascade
---------------------------------------------------------
Baroclinic life cycles in the real atmosphere proceed through: (1) linear
instability and growth of Rossby wave modes, (2) nonlinear wave saturation and
breaking, (3) irreversible PV mixing across isentropes, (4) transfer of energy
from baroclinic eddies to the mean flow and to barotropic Rossby waves. This
sequence completes in approximately 7–14 days and recurs throughout the extratrop-
ical storm tracks.

FCNv2 has learned the statistical outcome of this cycle from the ERA5 training
data, but cannot reproduce the underlying nonlinear dynamics. The AFNO architecture
operates globally at the same resolution for all wavenumbers — it does not perform
the multiscale cascade that characterizes turbulent atmospheric flows. Over 60
days, multiple baroclinic life cycles should occur; the model's inability to
correctly simulate these cycles is the deepest source of its mature-phase bias.

The consequence is visible in the spatial structure of the Z500 bias: the model's
largest errors (reaching -9,389 m2/s2 locally) are not distributed randomly but
concentrate in the storm track regions where baroclinic activity is highest and
nonlinear wave breaking is most frequent. These are exactly the regions where the
statistical autoregressive approach breaks down most severely.

3.4  Conservation laws absent from the ML framework
----------------------------------------------------
The following conservation laws are explicitly enforced in dynamical core NWP
models but are NOT encoded in FCNv2's training objective:

(a) Mass conservation: integral of density dV = constant
    Evidence: FCN can develop Z500 biases that imply mass redistribution beyond
    what surface pressure changes would allow.

(b) Energy conservation: d/dt(KE + APE) = diabatic source - dissipation
    Evidence: the systematic Wind500 bias of -1.01 m/s and negative Z500 mean
    bias indicate a slow, net kinetic energy loss from the model atmosphere.
    A physical model's dissipation is balanced by thermal forcing; FCNv2's
    "dissipation" is the implicit smoothing of its spectral representation.

(c) Angular momentum conservation: the mean westerly flow is maintained in NWP
    by the mountain torque and surface stress balance. FCNv2's near-zero U500
    mean bias (-0.03 m/s) suggests this constraint is approximately satisfied
    in a statistical sense, but the large spatial errors indicate the zonal-
    asymmetric component of the flow is unconstrained.

3.5  Radiative-dynamical feedback and the "climate drift"
---------------------------------------------------------
Over timescales beyond ~2 weeks, the real atmospheric circulation is shaped by
the radiative equilibrium temperature profile and the diabatic heating from the
ocean, land surface, and precipitation. Without explicit radiative transfer or
land-surface coupling, FCNv2's atmosphere has no mechanism to relax back toward
radiative-convective equilibrium when it drifts away. The -0.52 K T500 mean bias
and -14.7 m Z500 mean bias are early signatures of this "climate drift" — if the
model were run for months, the biases would grow further until the model settled
into its own statistical equilibrium (the "model climate"), which differs from
the ERA5 climate because the ML-learned dynamics does not conserve the correct
energy and momentum budgets.

================================================================================
4. ML ARCHITECTURAL ANALYSIS: HOW FOURCASTNETV2'S DESIGN SHAPES THE BIAS
   STRUCTURE
================================================================================

4.1  The AFNO architecture and its spectral implications
--------------------------------------------------------
FourCastNetv2 employs the Adaptive Fourier Neural Operator (AFNO; Guibas et al.,
2021) within a Vision Transformer (ViT) backbone. The key computational step is:

  x_out = IFFT(W(k) * FFT(x_in))

where x is the 2D atmospheric field, FFT/IFFT are the Fast Fourier Transforms,
and W(k) is a learnable complex weight matrix for each wavenumber k. This
architecture mixes spatial information globally in spectral space, giving each
output grid point access to the global atmospheric state through the wavenumber
representation.

Consequence for bias structure: AFNO errors are spectrally coherent. A learned
weight W(k) that is slightly wrong for a given wavenumber does not produce a
local error — it produces a global, wavenumber-k-shaped error pattern that
projects onto all grid points simultaneously. This is why the Z500 bias extremes
are so large: a slight phase or amplitude error in the model's representation of
planetary waves (wavenumber 2–6) projects onto ~1,000–10,000 grid points, creating
the large-amplitude, spatially coherent bias patterns seen in Figure 5.43. In
contrast, a physical model's errors are local: a parameterization error at one
grid point affects only its neighborhood through horizontal diffusion and advection.

4.2  Auto-regressive error accumulation in spectral space
---------------------------------------------------------
FCNv2 performs 120 sequential forward passes (12-hr steps) over 60 days. Each
pass has an associated truncation error epsilon. In spectral space, the amplification
factor for wavenumber k after n steps is:

  |error_n(k)| = |epsilon(k)| * |lambda(k)|^n

where lambda(k) is the effective "growth rate" of perturbation mode k. For the
atmosphere, lambda < 1 for stable modes (planetary waves) and lambda > 1 for
growing modes (baroclinic eddies). FCNv2's learned W(k) must approximate these
spectral amplification factors from data.

The Z500 MAB of 588 m2/s2 after 60 days (120 steps), compared to approximately
~1,500–2,000 m2/s2 of typical Z500 spatial standard deviation in the real
atmosphere, represents ~30–40% normalized error. Assuming a roughly exponential
growth rate: 588/initial_error = exp(120 * gamma), where initial_error is the
typical Z500 error at forecast initialization. If the initial error is ~5 m2/s2
(excellent analysis), the error has grown by a factor of ~100 in 120 steps, implying
gamma ~ 0.038 per step or an e-folding time of ~26 steps = 13 days. This is
comparable to the atmospheric predictability time scale of ~2 weeks, suggesting
FCNv2's Z500 error growth rate is physically plausible but not better than the
theoretical limit, and certainly much worse than operational NWP models in the
2–4 week extended range.

4.3  Multi-head attention and teleconnections
---------------------------------------------
The ViT backbone uses multi-head self-attention to combine information across
all patch embeddings (each patch ~ 4x4 grid points in the 0.25-degree
representation). Attention heads are believed to capture teleconnection patterns
(e.g., PNA, NAO, ENSO teleconnections) by learning which distant regions covary
in the ERA5 training data.

The insertion experiment results provide indirect evidence about what these
attention heads represent:

  - Z500 insertion corrects T500 by 88%: strong Z-T attention heads exist that
    encode the hypsometric relationship globally.
  - Z500 insertion corrects Wind500 by 71%: Z-Wind attention heads encode
    geostrophic balance.
  - T500 insertion corrects Z500 by only 40%: the T→Z heads are weaker, suggesting
    the model has learned that Z determines T more strongly than T determines Z.
    This reflects the physical hierarchy: in the real atmosphere, Z is the more
    slowly-varying "memory" variable and T responds to it rapidly, not the reverse.

The 0% "all insertions worsen" area (Class 2 in Figure 5.47) is an important
finding: there is no region of the globe where all three insertions simultaneously
increase Z500 bias. This means the attention mechanism always provides at least
some correctly-directed response to any single-variable correction. The model's
failure mode is one of incomplete coupling, not of inverted coupling.

4.4  The 73-channel representation and channel coupling
-------------------------------------------------------
FCNv2 processes 73 channels simultaneously: 8 surface variables + 5 variables
(u, v, z, t, r) at 13 pressure levels (50–1000 hPa). The AFNO mixing operates
across all channels at each forward step, so information from one channel
propagates to all others.

The insertion experiments measure the effective cross-channel coupling strength:

  Coupling strength(A → B) = 1 - [MAB(B, with A inserted)] / [MAB(B, control)]

Key coupling strengths measured:
  Z500 → T500   : 0.882  (very strong, near hypsometric theoretical maximum)
  Z500 → U500   : 0.720  (strong, geostrophic balance)
  Z500 → V500   : 0.802  (strong, geostrophic balance, higher for V than U)
  Wind → Z500   : 0.652  (moderate, advection-vorticity pathway)
  T500 → Z500   : 0.403  (weaker, incomplete hypsometric inversion)
  T500 → U500   : 0.207  (weak, thermal wind)
  T500 → V500   : 0.302  (weak, thermal wind, higher for V)

The asymmetry between V500 coupling (higher) and U500 coupling (lower) in both
T→Wind and Z→Wind pathways suggests that the model represents the meridional
component of balanced flow better than the zonal component. This could reflect
a training bias: in the extratropics, V500 anomalies are more strongly correlated
with both Z500 gradients (through geostrophy) and T500 gradients (through thermal
wind) than U500, because the dominant zonal-wavenumber-2 to -4 Rossby wave
pattern induces stronger meridional flow variations than zonal flow variations.

4.5  The smoothing bias of the L2 loss function
------------------------------------------------
FCNv2 was trained using a mean-squared-error (MSE / L2) loss. From a statistical
perspective, minimizing E[||y_pred - y_true||^2] produces predictions that converge
to the conditional mean E[y_true | x_input] rather than to any single realization.
As forecast lead time increases, the conditional mean becomes smoother (uncertainty
grows, the support of the conditional distribution broadens), and the model's
predictions approach the climatological mean field.

This "regression to the mean" is the statistical mechanism behind:
(a) The Wind500 mean bias of -1.01 m/s (speeds underestimated, approaching
    climatological mean speed which is lower than instantaneous speeds)
(b) The T500 min bias of -26 K and max bias of +22 K (extreme temperature
    anomalies are systematically underestimated)
(c) The Z500 range collapse (observed range: 46,097–58,489 m2/s2; model range
    in mature phase is narrowed toward the mean)

The L2 loss creates a spectral penalty on high wavenumbers: sharp gradients have
high spatial frequency content, and the MSE penalty on these wavenumbers causes
the model to learn weights that smooth them. Over 120 autoregressive steps, this
smoothing compounds multiplicatively, leading to the progressive spectral reddening
(energy transfer from high to low wavenumbers) visible as the forecast extends
into the mature phase.

4.6  Implications of the failure-mode classification (Figure 5.47)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Area-weighted classification of Z500 failure modes:
  Class 0 - Neutral / mixed        : 38.84%
  Class 1 - All insertions improve : 32.04%
  Class 2 - All insertions worsen  :  0.00%
  Class 3 - Temp-insertion best    :  0.00%
  Class 4 - Wind-insertion best    :  0.00%
  Class 5 - Geo-insertion best     : 18.11%
  Class 6 - Temp-insertion worst   :  9.57%
  Class 7 - Wind-insertion worst   :  1.44%
  Class 8 - Geo-insertion worst    :  0.00%

The 32% "all insertions improve" class represents regions where the model's error
is well-distributed across multiple variables: the balanced dynamics are wrong, and
correcting any single variable helps. These are likely the main storm track regions
where baroclinic activity drives correlated T-Z-Wind errors.

The 18% "geo-insertion best alone" class is highly significant. These are regions
where temperature or wind insertion is not sufficient to correct Z500, but Z500
self-correction dramatically outperforms. These regions likely correspond to areas
where the model's Z500 error has been generated by processes entirely internal to
the geopotential field (e.g., spurious Rossby wave propagation, incorrect barotrop-
ic wave speeds) that cannot be corrected by constraining either T or Wind alone.
Physically, these are regions where the atmospheric dynamics are barotropic: the
Z500 pattern is driven by the integrated effect of upper-level divergence and
convergence from non-local processes rather than by the local T or V gradients.

The 9.57% "temp-insertion worst" class has the most profound implications for
data assimilation design. These are regions where inserting correct T500 worsens
the Z500 forecast. The mechanism is analysis shock: the model has developed an
internally consistent (though wrong) balanced state. Inserting truth T500 into
this state creates a thermal-dynamical imbalance. FCNv2 responds to this imbalance
not by the correct geostrophic adjustment (which requires rotating the full pressure-
wind field) but by a learned response that may move Z500 in the wrong direction
because the surrounding atmospheric state (winds, geopotential at other levels)
is still wrong. This is the ML equivalent of the well-known "analysis increment
shock" problem in variational data assimilation: increments applied without proper
balance constraints can temporarily worsen the forecast.

The 0% for Classes 2, 3, 4, and 8 is a reassuring result:
  - Class 2 = 0%: No region where ALL insertions worsen Z500 simultaneously.
    This means there is no "attractor trap" where the model is stuck in a state
    so wrong that any correct information makes things worse.
  - Class 3 = 0%, Class 4 = 0%: Temperature or wind insertion alone is never
    the best strategy. When it helps, it does so in combination with other
    improvements (Class 1), not in isolation.
  - Class 8 = 0%: Geopotential insertion never worsens Z500 globally. Given
    that Z-insertion sets Z500 to truth exactly, this confirms the trivial
    non-negativity property of the analysis.

5. SYNTHESIS: WHAT FCNv2 HAS AND HAS NOT LEARNED
================================================================================

HAS LEARNED (empirically demonstrated by insertion experiments):
  1. Geostrophic balance: Z500 → wind coupling at 72–80% efficiency
  2. Hypsometric relationship: Z500 → T500 coupling at 88% efficiency
  3. Horizontal advection of T and Z by the wind field (Wind→T: 41%, Wind→Z: 65%)
  4. Statistical thermal wind: T500 → Wind coupling at 21–30%
  5. The hierarchy of atmospheric state variables: Z500 is the "master variable"
     encoding more information about the atmospheric state than T or Wind alone
  6. Globally coherent teleconnection patterns via attention mechanism
  7. Short-to-medium range Rossby wave propagation (implicit in spectral weights)

HAS NOT LEARNED (revealed by persistent mature-phase errors):
  1. Potential vorticity conservation (leading to spurious PV anomalies)
  2. Geostrophic adjustment dynamics (no balance restoration after perturbation)
  3. Full nonlinear baroclinic wave life cycles (energy cascade failure)
  4. Mass and energy conservation (slow systematic drift)
  5. Radiative-dynamical equilibrium feedback (climate drift without restoring force)
  6. Multi-level hypsometric consistency (single-level T correction is insufficient
     to correct Z through the full column integral)
  7. Ageostrophic dynamics at fronts and in jet streaks (~20-28% residual wind error
     after Z-insertion)
  8. Correct balanced adjustment in dynamically inconsistent states (10% failure
     region of T-insertion)

6. IMPLICATIONS FOR DATA ASSIMILATION WITH ML MODELS
================================================================================

6.1  Optimal control variable for ML-based 4D-Var
--------------------------------------------------
The finding that Z500 corrects all other variables more efficiently than T500 or
Wind500 suggests that any variational data assimilation (3D-Var or 4D-Var) scheme
for FCNv2 should adopt Z500 (or geopotential at multiple levels) as the primary
control variable. This is consistent with standard NWP DA practice, where the
"balanced" control variable is a linearized streamfunction-like variable. For
FCNv2, this means that observation operators for temperature and wind should map
through a Z-centric balance operator to avoid the analysis shock pathways that
the univariate T-insertion reveals.

6.2  Multivariate vs. univariate insertion: the balance constraint
------------------------------------------------------------------
The 9.57% "temp-insertion worst" area quantifies the "analysis shock" risk from
univariate observation insertion. A proper ensemble Kalman filter (EnKF) or
4D-Var would propagate the T500 observation increment to Z500 and Wind500
simultaneously through the background error covariance matrix B. The B matrix
for FCNv2 should encode the coupling strengths measured in this analysis:

  B(Z500, T500) = O(0.88) * sigma(Z500) * sigma(T500)   [strong positive]
  B(Z500, U500) = O(0.72) * sigma(Z500) * sigma(U500)   [strong positive]
  B(T500, Z500) = O(0.40) * sigma(T500) * sigma(Z500)   [moderate]

These empirically-derived covariances could serve as the foundation for a
climatological background error covariance for ML-based ensemble DA.

6.3  Observation type ranking for extended-range applications
-------------------------------------------------------------
For extended-range (30–60 day) forecasts with FCNv2, the insertion experiments
suggest the following observing system priority:

  Priority 1: Geopotential height / thickness observations
    (radiosonde geopotential, GPS-RO refractivity, AMSU-A radiance that
    constrains tropospheric Z)
    → Corrects T by 88%, Wind by 71–80%, Z by 100%

  Priority 2: Wind observations (radiosonde winds, AMVs, Aeolus lidar winds)
    → Corrects Z by 65%, T by 41%

  Priority 3: Temperature observations (IASI, radiosonde T, ATMS brightness T)
    → Corrects Z by 40%, Wind by 21–30%, but with 10% risk of local worsening

This ranking differs from NWP practice in the medium range (1–10 days), where
temperature observations (infrared/microwave sounders) have historically been
the most impactful. For ML models with spectral dynamics centered on Z500, the
geopotential constraint is paramount.

6.4  Insertion frequency and the error doubling time
----------------------------------------------------
The e-folding time for Z500 error growth is approximately 13 days (Section 4.2),
similar to the atmospheric predictability limit. For an insertion cycle to be
effective, observations should be inserted at a frequency comparable to or shorter
than this time scale. The 12-hourly insertion cycle used in these experiments
is well within the error growth time scale and achieves dramatic corrections
(65–88%). A 6-day insertion cycle (the standard extended-range window) would
allow errors to grow by exp(6/13) ~ 1.6x between insertions and would be
significantly less effective. This has direct implications for the design of
future hybrid ML/NWP data assimilation cycles: frequent (6–12 hourly) state
correction is necessary to control ML model error growth in the extended range.

================================================================================
7. COMPARISON WITH EXISTING LITERATURE
================================================================================

The finding that FCNv2's Z500 MAB reaches ~588 m2/s2 (~60 m) at day 30–60 is
consistent with published evaluations of similar ML weather models. Pathak et al.
(2022, the original FourCastNet paper) showed that FourCastNet skill in Z500
drops to below climatology beyond approximately day 10–14, with RMSE exceeding
the climatological standard deviation. The mature-phase biases observed here are
therefore not surprising — they represent the known predictability horizon of
current ML weather models.

What this analysis adds beyond previous work is:
  (1) A quantitative decomposition of Z500 error into T-induced, wind-induced, and
      dynamically-internal components: 40% thermal, 65% kinematic, ~35% diabatic/
      structural residuals (with overlap from multiple corrections).
  (2) Empirical measurement of FCNv2's geostrophic coupling efficiency (72–80%)
      compared to the theoretical maximum of 100%.
  (3) Identification of the 10% failure region where T-insertion worsens Z500,
      providing a direct measurement of analysis shock risk for ML-based DA.
  (4) The finding that Z500 corrects T500 at 88% efficiency — higher than any
      other cross-variable coupling — which is not directly derivable from the
      standard RMSE evaluations published in the literature.

The comparison with Aurora (Microsoft's 1.3B-parameter model, shown separately
in Chapter 5) would be valuable: if Aurora's insertion experiments show higher
geostrophic coupling efficiency and smaller failure-mode areas, it would suggest
that model scale and architectural sophistication (Aurora uses 3D attention across
pressure levels) measurably improves the representation of atmospheric balance
relationships.

================================================================================
8. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

The Section 5.8 spatial bias analysis reveals a coherent picture of what
FourCastNetv2 has and has not learned about the physics of the mid-troposphere.
The model has internalized the three fundamental linear balance relationships of
quasi-geostrophic theory (hypsometric, geostrophic, thermal wind) with coupling
efficiencies of 40–88%, with geopotential height (Z500) emerging as the master
variable that best constrains the full atmospheric state. The dominant failure
mode is the accumulation of dynamically inconsistent atmospheric states in the
mature phase (days 30–60), driven by the absence of potential vorticity
conservation, geostrophic adjustment, and nonlinear wave-wave interactions in
the learned autoregressive dynamics.

The most actionable finding for data assimilation is the identification of the
optimal control variable (Z500) and the quantification of analysis shock risk from
univariate T-insertion (9.57% of global area). These results provide a physically
grounded foundation for designing observation-space and state-space corrections
for ML weather models in the extended range, and motivate the use of multivariate,
balanced increment approaches rather than simple channel-by-channel insertion.
