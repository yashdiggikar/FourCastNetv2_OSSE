------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.2  Time alignment: why only 00:00 and 12:00 UTC from ERA5
------------------------------------------------------------------------

ERA5 is 6-hourly: it has data at 00, 06, 12, 18 UTC every day.
FCN is 12-hourly: it has data only at 00 and 12 UTC every day.

To compare FCN forecast against ERA5 truth at the same moment in time,
ERA5 is subsetted to only the 00:00 and 12:00 UTC timesteps. The 06:00
and 18:00 UTC ERA5 data are discarded for this analysis.

Result: both FCN and ERA5 have exactly 120 matched timesteps
        (2023-02-01 00:00 to 2023-04-01 12:00, every 12 hours).

------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.3  Grid alignment: why no interpolation was needed
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Both FCN and ERA5 are on a 0.25-degree regular latitude-longitude grid:
  721 latitudes from 90.0 N to 90.0 S (step -0.25 degrees)
  1440 longitudes from 0.0 E to 359.75 E (step +0.25 degrees)

The grids are identical. No spatial interpolation is needed or performed.
The check confirmed: ERA5 lat array matches FCN lat array to within 0.01 deg.

1.4  Unit alignment
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Variable   FCN unit       ERA5 unit      Action
---------  -------------  -------------  ---------------------------------
T500       Kelvin (K)     Kelvin (K)     None needed. Confirmed by range:
                                         FCN 205–273 K, ERA5 222–278 K.
U500       m/s            m/s            None needed.
V500       m/s            m/s            None needed.
Z500       m2/s2          m2/s2          None needed.
                                         FCN range: 39,070–58,258 m2/s2
                                         ERA5 range: 45,949–58,488 m2/s2
                                         Both are geopotential, not
                                         geopotential height. Confirmed
                                         by magnitude (~55,000 m2/s2
                                         expected for 500 hPa).

NOTE on Z500 units:
  Geopotential (Phi, in m2/s2) and geopotential HEIGHT (Z, in meters) are
  related by: Z = Phi / g, where g = 9.80665 m/s2.
  At 500 hPa: Phi ~ 55,000 m2/s2, Z ~ 5,600 m.
  Both FCN and ERA5 store geopotential (m2/s2), so no division is needed.
  The bias values (e.g., MAB = 588 m2/s2) can be converted to meters of
  geopotential height by dividing by g: 588 / 9.80665 = 59.9 m.

PART II: MATURE PHASE SELECTION
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1  What is the "mature phase" and why use it?
------------------------------------------------------------------------

A weather forecast has two broad regimes:

  SPIN-UP phase (lead days 0 to ~30):
    The forecast is still close to its initial condition. Errors are
    growing but are still relatively small. The model is in transition
    from the initial state toward its own dynamical equilibrium.

  MATURE phase (lead day 30 onward):
    The forecast has drifted far enough from the initial condition that
    it is no longer "remembering" the IC. Errors are large and spatially
    structured. The atmosphere the model is simulating is now substantially
    different from the real atmosphere.

For Section 5.8 (spatial bias analysis), the mature phase is chosen because:
  - It reveals the model's systematic tendencies most clearly (biases
    driven by the model's physics, not by IC quality).
  - It is the most challenging regime for extended-range prediction.
  - Insertion experiments in the mature phase test whether truth data can
    rescue a degraded forecast — the most demanding test of insertion value.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.2  Exact definition used here
------------------------------------------------------------------------

  IC time     : 2023-02-01 00:00 UTC
  Mature start: IC + 30 days = 2023-03-03 00:00 UTC
  Mature end  : last available FCN step = 2023-04-01 12:00 UTC

  Number of mature-phase steps: 60
  (FCN steps 60 through 119 of the 120-step forecast)

  Lead times at mature phase start and end:
    Step 60:  lead time = 60 x 12 hr = 720 hr = 30.0 days
    Step 119: lead time = 119 x 12 hr = 1428 hr = 59.5 days

  ERA5 mature-phase timesteps: the same 60 times (00 and 12 UTC,
    2023-03-03 to 2023-04-01 12:00). Only March and April ERA5 files
    are needed (the February file is not used for bias calculation).

PART III: BIAS FORMULA AND CALCULATION
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1  Spatial mean bias formula
------------------------------------------------------------------------

For a single variable V, the spatial mean bias at grid point (lat, lon) is:

  bias(lat, lon) = (1/N) * SUM over t from t1 to tN of [FCN(t,lat,lon) - ERA5(t,lat,lon)]

where:
  N       = number of mature-phase timesteps = 60
  t1      = first mature-phase time = 2023-03-03 00:00 UTC
  tN      = last mature-phase time  = 2023-04-01 12:00 UTC
  FCN(t,lat,lon) = FCN forecast value at time t, latitude lat, longitude lon
  ERA5(t,lat,lon) = ERA5 truth value at the same time, lat, lon

The result is a 2D array of shape (721 lat, 1440 lon) with units matching
the variable (K for T500, m/s for U500/V500/Wind500, m2/s2 for Z500).

Plain-language meaning:
  bias(lat, lon) = at this specific location, on average over all 60
  mature-phase timesteps, how far above or below truth is the FCN forecast?

  Positive bias: FCN is systematically too high (too warm, too fast, etc.)
  Negative bias: FCN is systematically too low (too cold, too slow, etc.)
  Zero bias: FCN is unbiased at this location (but may still have large
             random errors cancelling each other out)

------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.2  Mean bias (global scalar)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

  mean_bias = (1 / (Nlat x Nlon)) * SUM over all (lat,lon) of bias(lat,lon)

This is the spatially-averaged bias — a single number summarizing whether
the model is globally too high or too low for this variable.

Values computed for control run:
  T500    mean bias = -0.52 K         (slight global cold bias)
  U500    mean bias = -0.03 m/s       (effectively zero mean)
  V500    mean bias = +0.04 m/s       (effectively zero mean)
  Z500    mean bias = -144.7 m2/s2    (systematic low-pressure bias, ~14.7 m)
  Wind500 mean bias = -1.01 m/s       (slight underestimation of wind speed)

NOTE: Mean bias close to zero does NOT mean the model is accurate. It means
the positive and negative errors cancel each other spatially. The model may
still be very wrong locally (see MAB below).

------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.3  Mean Absolute Bias (MAB)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

  MAB = (1 / (Nlat x Nlon)) * SUM over all (lat,lon) of |bias(lat,lon)|

This is the spatially-averaged ABSOLUTE bias. It does NOT allow positive and
negative errors to cancel. It measures the typical size of the bias regardless
of sign.

  MAB values for control run:
    T500    MAB = 2.20 K
    U500    MAB = 4.68 m/s
    V500    MAB = 3.37 m/s
    Z500    MAB = 588.4 m2/s2   (= 60.0 geopotential meters after /g)
    Wind500 MAB = 3.47 m/s

Interpretation:
  T500: At a typical location on the map, the FCN forecast temperature is
        about 2.2 K away from truth during the mature phase.
  Z500: At a typical location, geopotential is about 588 m2/s2 (60 m) away
        from truth — this is very large. The climatological standard deviation
        of Z500 is about 1,500–2,000 m2/s2, so the MAB is roughly 30–40% of
        natural variability. The forecast has essentially lost phase coherence
        with the real atmospheric circulation.
  U500: Typical zonal wind errors of 4.68 m/s on a background of ~20 m/s
        average jet-stream winds = about 23% error in wind magnitude.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.4  Derived variable: Wind500
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Wind500 is the 500 hPa horizontal wind speed (scalar speed, not a vector).

  Formula:
    Wind500(t, lat, lon) = sqrt( U500(t,lat,lon)^2 + V500(t,lat,lon)^2 )

This is computed independently for FCN and ERA5 at every timestep and
grid point, and THEN biased:

  bias_Wind500(lat,lon) = (1/N) * SUM_t [ sqrt(U_fcn^2 + V_fcn^2)
                                         - sqrt(U_era5^2 + V_era5^2) ]

This is NOT the same as sqrt(bias_U^2 + bias_V^2). The bias of a
nonlinear function is not the function of the individual biases.

Why use Wind500 and not just U500 and V500 separately?
  Wind speed captures the magnitude of the flow regardless of direction.
  A model might have zero U500 bias and zero V500 bias but still have a
  Wind500 bias if the direction errors cause speed underestimation.

================================================================================
PART IV: BIAS REDUCTION FORMULA AND CALCULATION
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1  Bias reduction definition
------------------------------------------------------------------------

For each insertion experiment (T-ins, Wind-ins, Z-ins), bias reduction
measures how much the insertion improved the bias compared to the control:

  bias_reduction(lat, lon) = |control_bias(lat,lon)| - |insertion_bias(lat,lon)|

Expanded:
  bias_reduction(lat,lon) = |bias_control(lat,lon)| - |bias_insertion(lat,lon)|

where:
  bias_control(lat,lon)   = mean mature-phase bias of the CONTROL run
  bias_insertion(lat,lon) = mean mature-phase bias of the INSERTION run
  | | denotes absolute value

------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.2  Sign convention and interpretation
------------------------------------------------------------------------

  POSITIVE bias_reduction(lat,lon) means:
    |insertion_bias| < |control_bias|
    The insertion reduced the size of the bias at this location.
    The model improved here. The absolute error is smaller.

  NEGATIVE bias_reduction(lat,lon) means:
    |insertion_bias| > |control_bias|
    The insertion increased the size of the bias at this location.
    The model got WORSE here despite inserting truth data.
    This is the "failure mode" region.

  ZERO means:
    The insertion had no effect on the bias magnitude at this location.

Why use absolute values |...|?
  Consider a control bias of +5 at a point.
  After insertion, the bias becomes -3 at the same point.
  Without absolute values: "improvement" = 5 - (-3) = 8 (misleadingly large).
  With absolute values: |5| - |-3| = 5 - 3 = 2 (correct: the error
  shrank from 5 to 3 in magnitude, so 2 units of improvement).

  The absolute value correctly captures the reduction in error magnitude
  regardless of whether the sign of the bias changed.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.3  Mean bias reduction (global scalar)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

  mean_bias_reduction = spatial mean of bias_reduction(lat,lon)

Positive mean = insertion improved the average bias globally.
Negative mean = insertion worsened the average bias globally (would be
               a catastrophic result — this did not happen for any
               insertion in this experiment).

Values computed:
  T-ins  vs control:
    T500    mean BR = +2.202  K         (perfect: inserted = truth)
    U500    mean BR = +0.970  m/s
    V500    mean BR = +1.018  m/s
    Z500    mean BR = +237.1  m2/s2
    Wind500 mean BR = +0.490  m/s

  Wind-ins vs control:
    T500    mean BR = +0.903  K
    U500    mean BR = +4.681  m/s       (perfect: inserted = truth)
    V500    mean BR = +3.370  m/s       (perfect: inserted = truth)
    Z500    mean BR = +383.6  m2/s2
    Wind500 mean BR = +3.466  m/s       (perfect: inserted = truth)

  Z-ins vs control:
    T500    mean BR = +1.941  K
    U500    mean BR = +3.371  m/s
    V500    mean BR = +2.704  m/s
    Z500    mean BR = +588.4  m2/s2     (perfect: inserted = truth)
    Wind500 mean BR = +2.445  m/s

NOTE on "perfect" (MAB = 0.0000):
  When a variable is inserted (replaced with ERA5 truth at every step),
  its own bias becomes exactly zero. The model output for that channel
  equals truth by construction. This confirms the insertion worked as
  intended — the infrastructure is correct.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.4  Percentage bias reduction
------------------------------------------------------------------------

To compare across variables with different units and scales:

  pct_reduction = 100 * mean_bias_reduction / control_MAB

Values:
  T-ins  effect on Z500  : 100 * 237.1  / 588.4  = 40.3%
  Wind-ins effect on Z500: 100 * 383.6  / 588.4  = 65.2%
  Z-ins  effect on T500  : 100 * 1.941  / 2.202  = 88.2%
  Z-ins  effect on U500  : 100 * 3.371  / 4.681  = 72.0%
  Z-ins  effect on V500  : 100 * 2.704  / 3.370  = 80.2%
  Z-ins  effect on Wind500: 100 * 2.445 / 3.466  = 70.5%
  T-ins  effect on U500  : 100 * 0.970  / 4.681  = 20.7%
  Wind-ins effect on T500: 100 * 0.903  / 2.202  = 41.0%

These percentages are used in the theoretical analysis to quantify which
physical balance relationship is best represented in FCNv2.

================================================================================
PART V: FAILURE MODE CLASSIFICATION
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1  Definition of "improvement" for failure-mode map
------------------------------------------------------------------------

For the failure-mode map (Figure 5.47), the diagnostic variable is Z500.
At each grid point, three improvement scores are computed:

  temp_improvement(lat,lon) = |ctrl_Z500_bias(lat,lon)|
                              - |t_ins_Z500_bias(lat,lon)|

  wind_improvement(lat,lon) = |ctrl_Z500_bias(lat,lon)|
                              - |wind_ins_Z500_bias(lat,lon)|

  geo_improvement(lat,lon)  = |ctrl_Z500_bias(lat,lon)|
                              - |z_ins_Z500_bias(lat,lon)|

Each value:
  > 0: this insertion reduced the Z500 bias at this point (improved)
  < 0: this insertion increased the Z500 bias at this point (worsened)
  = 0: no change

------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.2  Threshold
------------------------------------------------------------------------

To avoid classifying tiny differences as meaningful:

  threshold = 0.10 * 90th_percentile_of( |ctrl_Z500_bias| )

From the computed data:
  90th percentile of |ctrl_Z500_bias| = 1460.86 m2/s2
  threshold = 0.10 * 1460.86 = 146.09 m2/s2

A grid point is only classified as "improved" or "worsened" if the
improvement/worsening exceeds 146 m2/s2 in magnitude. Below this
threshold the point is classified as "Neutral / mixed" (Class 0).

Why 10% of the 90th percentile?
  The 90th percentile is a robust measure of the large-bias regions where
  classification is most meaningful. Taking 10% of it as threshold filters
  out noise while retaining meaningful signal. This is a standard choice
  for classification thresholds in NWP evaluation studies.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.3  Classification logic
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Step 1: Compute temp_improvement, wind_improvement, geo_improvement.
Step 2: Find which insertion has the BEST (largest) improvement: best_idx.
Step 3: Find which insertion has the WORST (most negative) improvement: worst_idx.
Step 4: Classify each grid point into one of 9 classes:

  Class 0: Neutral / mixed
    All three improvements are within [-threshold, +threshold].
    No insertion clearly helps or hurts.
    Interpretation: Z500 bias at this location is not strongly
    responsive to any single variable correction.

  Class 1: All insertions improve
    temp_improvement > threshold AND
    wind_improvement > threshold AND
    geo_improvement  > threshold
    Interpretation: Z500 error at this location has contributions from
    all three variables. Any insertion helps because the underlying
    dynamics are correlated across T, Wind, and Z.
    This is the "well-coupled" class — the model has wrong balanced dynamics.

  Class 2: All insertions worsen
    temp_improvement < -threshold AND
    wind_improvement < -threshold AND
    geo_improvement  < -threshold
    Interpretation: This would mean that at these points, inserting any
    truth variable makes the Z500 forecast WORSE. This class was 0.0%
    area in this experiment — a reassuring result meaning there is no
    region where the model is so badly wrong that truth information hurts.

  Class 3: Temperature insertion best (alone)
    best_idx = temp, max_improvement > threshold,
    NOT in Class 1.
    Interpretation: At these points, T500 correction is the most
    effective single-variable fix for Z500 bias. The Z500 error is
    primarily thermally driven (temperature gradient errors).

  Class 4: Wind insertion best (alone)
    best_idx = wind, max_improvement > threshold.
    Interpretation: Wind advection errors are the primary driver of
    Z500 bias here.

  Class 5: Geopotential insertion best (alone) — 18.1% of globe
    best_idx = geo, max_improvement > threshold,
    NOT in Class 1.
    Interpretation: At these points, correcting Z500 itself is the
    only effective fix, and T or Wind correction is insufficient.
    These are regions where the Z500 error is "internally generated"
    by the model's dynamical inconsistencies rather than driven by
    T or Wind errors. Typically: barotropic flow regimes, dipole
    blocking patterns, strong planetary wave anomalies that are
    purely geopotential-driven.

  Class 6: Temperature insertion worst — 9.57% of globe
    worst_idx = temp, min_improvement < -threshold.
    Interpretation: Inserting correct T500 makes the Z500 bias WORSE
    at these locations. This is the "analysis shock" class.
    When T500 is anchored to truth but Z500 and Wind are wrong, the
    model tries to "balance" the inconsistent state, and the resulting
    Z500 change goes in the wrong direction.

  Class 7: Wind insertion worst — 1.44% of globe
    worst_idx = wind, min_improvement < -threshold.
    Interpretation: Similar to Class 6 but for wind insertion.
    Less common because wind correction more directly fixes
    geopotential through advection, making "wrong direction"
    responses rarer.

  Class 8: Geopotential insertion worst — 0.0% of globe
    worst_idx = geo, min_improvement < -threshold.
    This class is always empty because Z-insertion sets Z500 to truth
    exactly, making its own Z500 bias zero. The bias reduction for
    Z-insertion vs. Z500 is always >= 0 by mathematical construction.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.4  Area-weighted percentages
------------------------------------------------------------------------

A simple area average would overweight the poles (where grid cells are
physically small despite having the same lat-lon extent as equatorial cells).
Cosine-latitude weighting corrects for this:

  weight(lat) = cos( lat * pi/180 )

The weight is highest at the equator (weight = 1) and zero at the poles
(weight = 0), proportional to the true physical area of each latitude band.

  area_pct(class k) = 100 * [ SUM over all (lat,lon) where class=k of weight(lat) ]
                            / [ SUM over all (lat,lon) of weight(lat) ]

This gives the true fractional AREA of the globe in each class, accounting
for the spherical geometry of the Earth.

Results (area-weighted):
  Class 0 - Neutral / mixed        : 38.84%
  Class 1 - All insertions improve : 32.04%
  Class 2 - All insertions worsen  :  0.00%
  Class 3 - Temp-insertion best    :  0.00%
  Class 4 - Wind-insertion best    :  0.00%
  Class 5 - Geo-insertion best     : 18.11%
  Class 6 - Temp-insertion worst   :  9.57%
  Class 7 - Wind-insertion worst   :  1.44%
  Class 8 - Geo-insertion worst    :  0.00%

WHAT IT SHOWS:

    Row 2: U500 bias (m/s)       — zonal wind error map
    Row 3: Z500 bias (m2/s2)     — geopotential error map

    Blue  = negative bias (FCN is too low/cold/slow at that location)

  The colormap limits are set symmetrically to the 97th percentile of the
  absolute bias, so extreme outliers do not dominate the color scale.

WHAT TO LOOK FOR IN EACH PANEL:

  T500 bias (Row 1):
    - Are there large red or blue patches? → systematic warm/cold regions.
    - Do the patterns align with known features (polar vortex, tropics)?
    - Typical control T500 MAB = 2.20 K. A large blue patch of -26 K
      somewhere indicates a catastrophically misplaced polar vortex edge.
    - Blue (cold bias) in the polar regions would indicate the model's
      polar vortex has drifted equatorward or intensified unrealistically.
    - Red (warm bias) in the subtropics would indicate the subtropical jet
      is misplaced and advecting too much warm air.

  U500 bias (Row 2):
    - Alternating red-blue bands aligned with latitude → jet stream is
      displaced: if blue equatorward of the jet position and red poleward,
      the jet has shifted poleward in the model.
    - East-west alternation → the model's planetary wave pattern is out
      of phase with reality (the jet is undulating in a different location).
    - MAB = 4.68 m/s. Any region with bias > 10 m/s is severely wrong.

  Z500 bias (Row 3):
    - This is the most important panel. Z500 encodes the entire large-scale
      circulation (troughs = low Z500, ridges = high Z500).
    - Continuous blue patches = persistent model troughs where there should
      be ridges, or too-deep troughs (the model's atmosphere is too cold there).
    - Continuous red patches = persistent model ridges where there should
      be troughs, or too-high pressure systems.
    - MAB = 588 m2/s2 (60 m). The range goes to -9,389 m2/s2 locally.
      Regions with |bias| > 2,000 m2/s2 are essentially in the wrong
      atmospheric pattern entirely.
    - The global mean bias of -144 m2/s2 (the whole map is slightly blue-
      shifted) indicates the model atmosphere is on average slightly too cold
      or too low-pressure globally.

  Wind500 bias (Row 4):
    - Red = FCN is overestimating wind speed here.
    - Blue = FCN is underestimating wind speed (the more common error).
    - The global mean bias of -1.01 m/s means the model is systematically
      too slow everywhere. This is the "spectral smoothing" effect of the
      L2 training loss.
    - Strong negative (blue) patches in the jet stream latitude bands indicate
      the model has weakened the extratropical jet cores.

OVERALL INTERPRETATION OF FIGURE 5.43:
  This figure is the "fingerprint" of FCNv2's systematic failure modes in
  the mature phase. Any region that is strongly red or blue is a location
  where the model's autoregressive dynamics have caused the forecast to
  drift systematically away from reality. These patterns repeat case after
  case in the mature phase — they are not random errors, they are structural
  biases encoded in the learned neural network weights.

  Row 1 (T500):
    Control bias (col 1): T500 error map (should look the same as in Fig 5.43).
    Wind-ins bias (col 2): T500 errors after wind correction. MAB reduces
      from 2.20 K to 1.30 K (41% improvement). The remaining 1.30 K is the
      T500 error that cannot be fixed by winds alone — it comes from the
      model's thermodynamic (heating/cooling) errors.
    Bias reduction (col 3): Where wind insertion MOST helps T500 (red patches)
      tends to be in the storm track regions and jet exit zones where
      temperature advection by the wind is the dominant process.

  Row 2 (Z500):
    Control bias (col 1): Z500 error map.
    Wind-ins bias (col 2): Z500 errors after wind correction. MAB reduces from
      588 m2/s2 to 205 m2/s2 (65% improvement). The remaining 205 m2/s2 is
      the Z500 error from non-advective sources (diabatic heating, pressure
      tendency from temperature errors).
    Bias reduction (col 3): Should show the most improvement in regions with
      strong Z500 anomalies being advected by the jet — the storm track regions
      over the North Atlantic and North Pacific where advection dominates the
      Z500 budget.

KEY COMPARISON — Figures 5.44 vs 5.45:
  Wind insertion (Fig 5.45) reduces Z500 bias by 65% — FAR more than
  temperature insertion (Fig 5.44) which reduces Z500 bias by only 40%.
  This is the key result showing that, in FCNv2's mature phase, Z500 errors
  are primarily driven by incorrect wind advection, not incorrect temperature.

WHAT TO LOOK FOR:

    Control bias (col 1): T500 error (same as before).

    Bias reduction (col 3): Almost entirely red (Z500 correction benefits

      the 12% of T500 error that cannot be corrected by Z500 information —

    Z-ins bias (col 2): Wind500 after Z500 correction. MAB drops from 3.47 m/s

KEY RESULT in Figure 5.46:
  This figure provides the single clearest demonstration that Z500 is the
  "master variable" in FCNv2. Correcting just Z500 at 500 hPa eliminates:

  More than any other single-variable insertion.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT SHOWS:
  A single global map with 9 discrete colors, each color representing one
  failure-mode class (see Section 5.3 above for class definitions).

COLOR KEY:
  Gray   (Class 0): Neutral / mixed — no strong signal             38.84%
  Green  (Class 1): All insertions improve                         32.04%
  Dark red (Class 2): All insertions worsen                         0.00%
  Blue   (Class 3): Temperature insertion is uniquely best          0.00%
  Purple (Class 4): Wind insertion is uniquely best                 0.00%

  Dark purple (Class 7): Wind insertion is uniquely worst           1.44%
  Brown  (Class 8): Geopotential insertion is uniquely worst        0.00%

WHAT TO LOOK FOR:

  GREEN regions (32% of globe):
    Where you see green, the model's Z500 error has a balanced structure:
    all three insertions help because the T, Wind, and Z errors are
    dynamically coupled at those locations.
    These are typically the main baroclinic storm track regions
    (North Atlantic, North Pacific, Southern Ocean) where the atmosphere
    is most dynamically active and variable coupling is strongest.

  ORANGE regions (18% of globe):
    Where only Z-insertion helps, while T and Wind insertions are
    insufficient. These are regions with barotropic or quasi-stationary
    circulation anomalies that the model has generated purely through
    Z500 dynamics, decoupled from local T or Wind errors.
    Look for these in the subtropics (blocking anticyclones), the polar
    regions (vortex anomalies), or over the continents (stationary waves).

  DARK BLUE regions (10% of globe):
    The danger zone — where inserting truth temperature makes the Z500
    forecast WORSE. This is the most important failure region for
    understanding ML data assimilation limitations.
    These regions likely correspond to: frontal zones, jet stream exit
    regions, subtropical anticyclone edges — anywhere where the atmosphere
    requires a balanced multivariate correction rather than a single-
    variable fix. The model creates a dynamical inconsistency when T is
    corrected without simultaneously correcting the winds and Z at other
    levels.

  GRAY regions (39% of globe):
    These are "silent" regions where insertion effects are small but not
    clearly directional. Either the model has small bias here already, or
    the model's coupling is weak and inserting single variables has little
    effect. These may be regions of weak mid-tropospheric dynamics (tropical
    belt, subtropical high pressure regions) or regions where the model's
    errors are already below the classification threshold.

IMPORTANT NOTE on absent classes (0%, 0%, 0%, 0%):
  Classes 2, 3, 4, 8 are all zero area. This tells you:
    - Class 2 = 0%: GOOD. No region is so badly wrong that all three
      insertions make it worse. The model never enters a "trap" state.
    - Class 3 = 0%, Class 4 = 0%: Temp-only or Wind-only is never the
      uniquely best strategy. When these help uniquely, they do so in
      combination (Class 1) not in isolation.
    - Class 8 = 0%: Expected. Z-insertion cannot worsen its own target
      variable.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.1  Individual bias files: FCN_{run}_{var}_bias.csv
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Columns: lat, lon, bias
Rows   : 721 x 1440 = 1,038,240 rows (one per global grid point)

lat: latitude in degrees (-90 to 90)
lon: longitude in degrees (0 to 359.75)
bias: mean bias (FCN - ERA5) over the 60 mature-phase steps, in variable units

Example rows from FCN_control_z500_bias.csv:
  lat,   lon,  bias
  90.0,  0.00, -154.3
  90.0,  0.25, -152.7
  ...

How to use this file:
  - Load in Python (pd.read_csv), NumPy, Excel, or any spreadsheet.
  - To reconstruct the 2D map: df.pivot(index='lat', columns='lon', values='bias')
  - To compute MAB: df['bias'].abs().mean()
  - To find worst locations: df.nsmallest(10, 'bias') or df.nlargest(10, 'bias')

------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.2  Combined files: FCN_{run}_{var}_combined.csv
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Columns: lat, lon, control_bias, insertion_bias, bias_reduction

control_bias   : mean(control_FCN - ERA5) at this point
insertion_bias : mean(insertion_FCN - ERA5) at this point
bias_reduction : |control_bias| - |insertion_bias|

Positive bias_reduction = insertion helped (improved) at this point.
Negative bias_reduction = insertion hurt (worsened) at this point.

Example: FCN_t_ins_z500_combined.csv
  lat, lon, control_bias, insertion_bias, bias_reduction
  45.0, 270.0, -850.2, -510.1, 340.1    ← insertion reduced Z500 error by 340
  45.0, 270.25, -862.5, -915.3, -52.8   ← insertion slightly worsened Z500 here

------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.3  Failure mode map: FCN_failure_mode_map.csv
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Columns: lat, lon, class_id, class_name

class_id   : integer 0–8 (see classification table in Section 5.3)
class_name : text label (e.g., "All insertions improve", "Neutral / mixed")

This file contains 1,038,240 rows. You can subset it to find specific classes:
  df_class1 = df[df['class_id'] == 1]   # all "all improve" grid points
  df_worst_T = df[df['class_id'] == 6]  # temp-insertion worsens Z500

7.4  Area summary: FCN_failure_mode_area_summary.csv
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Columns: class_id, class_name, area_pct

9 rows, one per class.
area_pct is the cosine-latitude-weighted fractional area percentage.

This is the small summary table for reporting in thesis tables or figures.

================================================================================
PART VIII: QUICK REFERENCE — FORMULA SUMMARY
================================================================================

  BIAS:
    bias(lat,lon) = (1/N) * SUM_t [FCN(t,lat,lon) - ERA5(t,lat,lon)]
    N = 60 (mature phase timesteps)

  MEAN BIAS (global):
    mean_bias = mean over all (lat,lon) of bias(lat,lon)

  MEAN ABSOLUTE BIAS (MAB):
    MAB = mean over all (lat,lon) of |bias(lat,lon)|

  WIND SPEED:
    Wind500(t,lat,lon) = sqrt(U500^2 + V500^2)  [computed before bias]

  BIAS REDUCTION:
    bias_red(lat,lon) = |control_bias(lat,lon)| - |insertion_bias(lat,lon)|
    Positive = improved, Negative = worsened

  PERCENT REDUCTION:
    pct = 100 * mean(bias_red) / control_MAB

  FAILURE MODE THRESHOLD:
    threshold = 0.10 * percentile_90( |control_bias_Z500| )
    = 0.10 * 1460.86 = 146.09 m2/s2

  AREA-WEIGHTED PERCENTAGE:
    area_pct(class k) = 100 * SUM_{class=k}[cos(lat)] / SUM_all[cos(lat)]

  UNIT CONVERSION (Z500):
    geopotential (m2/s2) / 9.80665 = geopotential height (m)
    e.g., MAB = 588.4 m2/s2 = 60.0 geopotential meters
