1.  WHY LATITUDE MATTERS FOR ML WEATHER FORECAST ERROR
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A global atmospheric model encodes physics implicitly through training data
statistics. The atmosphere, however, operates under strongly latitude-dependent
dynamics:

  • Tropics (|lat| < 30°): Dominated by convective instability, diabatic
    heating, and the ITCZ. The Rossby number is O(1) or larger, so geostrophic
    balance does NOT hold. Energy cascades are dominated by convective towers
    and equatorial waves (Kelvin, Rossby, MJO). Predictability is limited by
    convective organisation, typically 1–2 weeks.

  • Midlatitudes (30°–60°): Baroclinic instability drives synoptic cyclone
    growth. The quasi-geostrophic (QG) framework applies: thermal wind balance
    links horizontal temperature gradients to vertical wind shear.
    Predictability in this regime extends ~2 weeks for synoptic systems.
    Baroclinic eddies transfer energy from the mean flow to growing disturbances.

  • Polar regions (|lat| > 60°): Strong planetary boundary layer effects,
    sea-ice interactions, and stratospheric coupling. The flow is nearly
    barotropic. Long Rossby wave propagation dominates interannual variability.
    FCNv2 sees relatively sparse training data at high latitudes due to
    polar night and the scarcity of radiosonde observations assimilated into
    ERA5.

An AFNO model trained on ERA5 learns spatial correlations across these regimes
simultaneously. However, the spectral basis functions (Fourier modes) used in
AFNO are global — they do not have an inherent latitude-dependent weighting.
This means errors in the highly energetic midlatitude baroclinic zone can
project onto tropical and polar Fourier modes, causing cross-band error
contamination not present in classical NWP models.

2.  EXPECTED RMSE PROFILES BY LATITUDE BAND
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

2.1  T500 RMSE by latitude:
──────────────────────────
  Tropics: Low initial RMSE (tropical temperature is relatively stable),
  slow growth (convective parameterisation not active in FCNv2), eventual
  plateau. Insertion of any variable should have modest tropical T500 impact
  because FCNv2 cannot represent diabatic processes.

  Midlatitudes: High initial RMSE growth rate due to baroclinic instability.
  The mature phase RMSE is dominated by the model's inability to correctly
  simulate cyclone lifecycle beyond predictability limit. Temperature insertion
  directly corrects T500; Z-insertion corrects T500 indirectly via hypsometric
  balance. Wind insertion's T500 correction is weakest here because
  thermal-wind is not the limiting error.

  Polar: Moderate RMSE, growth is slower. Corrections propagate more slowly
  due to lower phase speeds. The model likely has systematic cold/warm biases
  at high latitudes from ERA5 training data characteristics.

2.2  Z500 RMSE by latitude:
───────────────────────────
  Z500 is the master dynamical variable. Its RMSE characterises how well the
  model tracks the large-scale circulation pattern.

  Tropics: Z500 variations are small (tropical troposphere is nearly
  barotropic). RMSE is numerically small in absolute terms but percentage
  errors can be large. Insertion of Z500 should provide a large fractional
  improvement in the tropics.

  Midlatitudes: Largest absolute Z500 RMSE because amplitude of ridge-trough
  systems is 200–800 m²/s². Rapid error growth during baroclinic life cycle.
  Z-insertion provides the largest absolute improvement here. T-insertion
  corrects Z500 via hypsometric balance: ΔZ500 ~ (R/g) × Δ(T̄) × Δ(ln p).

  Polar: Z500 errors are dominated by Rossby wave propagation from midlatitude
  source regions. Corrections introduced at midlatitudes propagate poleward,
  producing lagged improvements in polar Z500.

2.3  Wind500 (U500, V500) RMSE by latitude:
────────────────────────────────────────────
  The thermal-wind relationship connects ∂u/∂p to ∂T/∂y. In midlatitudes
  where this balance is approximately maintained, T-insertion should reduce
  U500 RMSE significantly. In the tropics where the balance breaks down,
  T-insertion's effect on winds is weaker or absent.

  Wind-insertion directly corrects U500 and V500 in the midlatitudes where
  baroclinic instability drives large wind errors. Tropical impact is limited
  by the dominance of convective, not geostrophic, dynamics.

2.4  Wind10m RMSE by latitude:
───────────────────────────────
  Near-surface winds are influenced by the boundary layer (not represented in
  FCNv2's 500 hPa insertion experiments) and by surface friction, which varies
  greatly between ocean and land. Wind10m RMSE is expected to be:
  - Moderate in tropics (trade winds, relatively steady)
  - High over midlatitude storm tracks (rapid cyclone-induced gusts)
  - High in polar regions (katabatic winds, sea-ice edge dynamics)

  Upper-level insertions propagate to surface winds through geostrophic coupling,
  but this pathway is indirect and involves boundary-layer processes not
  explicitly represented in FCNv2.

3.  WHAT THE TIME SERIES STRUCTURE TELLS US
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

3.1  Two phases of RMSE growth:

  Phase 1 — IC information regime (day 0–~14):
    RMSE grows approximately as a power law. The inserted ERA5 truth provides
    a "corrected" starting point. In this phase, insertions should show the
    largest RMSE reduction because the forecast is still close to its IC.
    Latitude band differences reflect how quickly each regime "forgets" the IC.

  Phase 2 — Error saturation regime (day ~14–60):
    RMSE growth slows and the spread between runs narrows as all models lose
    skill. In the mature phase (day 30–60), RMSE reflects the intrinsic model
    climatological bias rather than IC sensitivity.

  The transition between phases is latitude-dependent:
  - Tropics: transition occurs later (~day 20–30) due to slower error growth
  - Midlatitudes: transition occurs ~day 10–15 (fast baroclinic instability)
  - Polar: transition occurs ~day 20–25 (slow Rossby wave propagation)

3.2  Cross-run comparison within a lat band:
    On each panel (one variable, one lat band), all 4 run lines are shown:
    - Separation between control and insertions quantifies information gain
    - Crossovers between insertion runs indicate regime-specific coupling
    - Lines converging in the mature phase → insertion has temporary effect only
    - Lines remaining separated in mature phase → insertion corrects systematic bias

3.3  Inter-band comparison:
    Comparing the same insertion effect across columns (Tropics / Midlats / Polar):
    - Larger separation in midlatitudes → insertion leverages baroclinic coupling
    - Larger separation in tropics → insertion targets tropical moisture or T gradient
    - Roughly equal impact across bands → insertion corrects a global systematic bias

4.  EXPECTED PHYSICAL RESULTS PER INSERTION TYPE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

4.1  Temperature Insertion (T-ins):

  Mechanism: By correcting T500 toward ERA5 truth, we restore the horizontal
  temperature gradients that drive thermal-wind balance and vertical motion
  through the omega equation.

  Expected lat-band response:
  - Midlatitudes: Strongest RMSE reduction for U500 and Z500 via thermal-wind.
    The QG height tendency equation: ∂Z/∂t = -f₀⁻¹ (u·∇Z + f·∇Z) + ...
    includes diabatic heating via hypsometric coupling.
  - Tropics: Modest reduction. Tropical T500 gradients are weak; the
    temperature-wind coupling is weak without Coriolis.
  - Polar: Moderate reduction. Polar vortex stability depends on Arctic
    temperature anomalies; T-insertion may correct jet stream position.

  Physical interpretation of underperformance: "Analysis shock" — inserting
  a corrected temperature field into a model that has adjusted its other
  variables to be consistent with the wrong temperature creates imbalances.
  These imbalances generate spurious gravity waves that can temporarily
  increase RMSE before the model re-balances.

4.2  Wind Insertion (Wind-ins):

  Mechanism: Correcting U500 and V500 restores the horizontal momentum, which
  influences temperature advection (∂T/∂t = -u·∇T + ...) and pressure tendency
  (∂Z/∂t includes the geostrophic wind component).

  Expected lat-band response:
  - Midlatitudes: Largest Z500 and T500 response because midlatitude dynamics
    are momentum-controlled. Baroclinic instability requires correct wind shear.
  - Tropics: Limited T500 response because tropical temperature anomalies are
    driven by convection, not horizontal advection.
  - Polar: Moderate response via stratospheric-tropospheric coupling and
    planetary wave propagation.

4.3  Geopotential Insertion (Z-ins):

  Mechanism: Z500 represents the depth of the 500 hPa layer, which determines
  the geostrophic wind via: u_g = -(g/f₀)(∂Z/∂y), v_g = (g/f₀)(∂Z/∂x).
  Inserting Z500 restores both the geostrophic balance and the mass field.

  Expected lat-band response:
  - Midlatitudes: Simultaneous correction of T500 (hypsometric) and winds
    (geostrophic). Should have the broadest corrective effect.
  - Tropics: Strong Z500 correction but weak wind response (Coriolis near zero).
    However, tropical Z500 is tightly coupled to deep convective heating.
  - Polar: Correct representation of the polar vortex geopotential is critical
    for Arctic oscillation and stratospheric coupling.

  Key insight: Z-insertion is expected to be the "master correction" because Z500
  contains the integrated information about both temperature (hypsometric) and
  wind (geostrophic) simultaneously. This should show up as the largest % RMSE
  reduction across all latitude bands for all diagnostic variables.

5.  ML ARCHITECTURE PERSPECTIVE ON LATITUDE-DEPENDENT ERRORS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

5.1  AFNO's spectral processing and latitude bias:
  The Adaptive Fourier Neural Operator processes atmospheric fields in
  spectral (wavenumber) space. The DFT assumes periodic, homogeneous fields.
  The real atmosphere is NOT homogeneous in latitude:
  - Meridional temperature gradient drives the jet stream
  - Rossby radius of deformation varies as ~N*H/f ∝ 1/sin(lat)
    (diverges at the equator)
  - The deformation radius at 60°N is ~3× smaller than at 30°N

  This means AFNO's fixed spectral basis is a natural fit for midlatitude
  synoptic systems (Rossby deformation radius ~1000 km) but a poor fit for:
  - Tropical mesoscale convection (100–500 km, non-geostrophic)
  - Polar vortex (hemispheric scale, wavenumber 1–3)

5.2  Training data imbalance:
  ERA5 training data represents 40 years of global reanalysis. Density of
  observational input to ERA5 is highest in the Northern Hemisphere midlatitudes
  (radiosondes, aircraft) and lowest over the Southern Ocean and Arctic.
  FCNv2 trained on this data likely learns better atmospheric dynamics in the
  data-rich midlatitudes than in polar or remote ocean regions.

  Implication for insertion experiments:
  - In midlatitudes where training data is dense, the FCNv2 internal dynamics
    are better calibrated. Insertions are more "compatible" with the model's
    learned physics. Recovery after insertion is faster and more stable.
  - In polar regions, the model may over-correct or show oscillatory behaviour
    after insertion because the model's dynamics are less well-constrained.

5.3  Cosine-latitude weighting effects:
  The global-mean error metrics used in many studies can be dominated by the
  relatively uniform tropical signal (large equatorial area, small RMSE).
  Cosine-latitude weighting gives more weight to higher-latitude regions where
  RMSE is larger. Without this weighting:
  - Tropical improvements would dominate the global signal
  - Polar improvements would be underrepresented (small area despite high error)
  Our analysis uses cosine weighting to give a physically meaningful average
  within each band, reflecting the atmospheric significance of errors.

5.4  Separation of FCNv2 errors by latitude:

  Structural errors (model cannot represent the physics):
  - Convective parameterisation: tropical tropics cannot be corrected
    by extratropical insertions
  - Boundary layer representation: affects wind10m everywhere

  Initialisation errors (poor IC, correctable by insertion):
  - Midlatitude baroclinic systems: correctable via Z500, T500, or wind insertion
  - Rossby wave trains: correctable via Z500 insertion at source region

  Accumulation errors (compound over time):
  - These appear as systematic RMSE growth in the mature phase
  - Latitude-specific trends in RMSE growth rate indicate where the model's
    internal dynamics diverge most rapidly from ERA5

7.  DYNAMICAL REGIME INTERPRETATION OF BAR CHART (Figure 5.39)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

7.1  Tropical insertion effectiveness:
  The tropics are dominated by convective organisation and moisture dynamics.
  None of the three insertion types (T500, wind500, Z500) directly corrects
  the convective parameterisation limitation of FCNv2. Therefore, even with
  perfect 500 hPa fields, tropical near-surface wind and precipitation errors
  will persist. This should show up as systematically smaller tropical bars
  in Figure 5.39 for wind10m, even if 500 hPa metrics improve.

7.2  Midlatitude insertion effectiveness:
  Midlatitudes are where all three insertion types should show the largest
  absolute improvement because:
  - Baroclinic instability is the main error source
  - Thermal-wind, geostrophic balance, and hypsometric relationships all hold
  - The AFNO model is best trained for this regime (densest ERA5 observations)
  Expected pattern: Z-ins > T-ins ≈ Wind-ins for Z500; Z-ins ≈ T-ins > Wind-ins
  for T500.

7.3  Polar insertion effectiveness:
  The polar region presents an interesting case. If FCNv2's polar dynamics are
  poorly constrained (due to sparse training data), insertions may show:
  - Larger absolute RMSE reduction (model was far from truth)
  - But also larger spread between runs (less stable corrections)
  Physically, correct polar Z500 is critical for representing the Arctic
  Oscillation (AO) and its teleconnection to midlatitude weather.
  If Z-insertion shows disproportionately large polar improvement, it confirms
  that FCNv2's polar error is primarily a geopotential height error driven by
  incorrect stratospheric-tropospheric coupling.

8.  COMPARISON WITH CLASSICAL NWP EXPECTATION
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

In classical NWP, latitude-dependent predictability is well-understood:
  - Tropics: ~1–2 weeks (convective limit)
  - Midlatitudes NH: ~10–14 days (baroclinic limit)
  - Midlatitudes SH: ~12–16 days (fewer observational errors in ERA5 IC)
  - Polar: ~2–3 weeks (slower dynamics, longer intrinsic timescales)

FCNv2's latitude-dependent error growth in the control run should roughly
match these classical expectations. Deviations from the classical picture
indicate where FCNv2 either outperforms NWP (likely in tropical wave
propagation which benefits from global spatial correlations learned by AFNO)
or underperforms (likely in tropical convection and polar stratospheric coupling).

The insertion experiments test whether these limitations are in the IC
(correctable) or in the model dynamics (not correctable by IC perturbations).
The latitude-band analysis allows us to identify which dynamical regime
drives the overall FCNv2 prediction error and where data assimilation efforts
(via insertion) are most effective.
